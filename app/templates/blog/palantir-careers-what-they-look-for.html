Palantir Careers: What They Look For + How to Apply

November 9, 2026

Palantir isn't a normal tech company. They don't sell software — they embed engineers inside client organizations. They don't have product managers in the traditional sense. And their hiring process is designed to find people who thrive in ambiguity, not people who need a spec sheet.

The Palantir Model

Palantir operates on a model called "Forward Deployment." Their engineers — called Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) — don't build products in a San Francisco office. They live inside client organizations (government agencies, hospitals, banks) and solve problems using Palantir's platforms (Foundry, AIP, Gotham).

This means every Palantir hire is evaluated on three axes:

  • Technical depth. Can you build complex data pipelines, ML systems, or security architectures from scratch?
  • Client fluency. Can you sit across from a CIA analyst, a NHS director, or a JP Morgan VP and understand their problem well enough to solve it?
  • Founder mentality. Palantir calls this "being a missionary, not a mercenary." They want people who treat every client engagement as if it were their own startup.

Where Palantir Posts Jobs

Palantir uses Lever as their ATS. Their board is at jobs.lever.co/palantir and is surprisingly comprehensive — everything from internships to senior staff roles across engineering, business development, and forward deployment.

The Palantir Interview

Palantir's interview process is infamous for being unpredictable. There is no standard loop. It varies by role, office, and interviewer. But the consistent threads are:

  1. Decomposition problem. "How many gas stations are there in the US?" or "How would you find a terrorist cell in a social network?" These aren't about the answer — they're about how you break down an ambiguous problem.
  2. Coding challenge. Palantir sends a take-home that usually involves building a small system. The evaluation isn't just "does it work" — it's "how did you think about trade-offs?"
  3. Founder interview. A conversation with a senior Palantir leader about why you want to join, what you've built, and how you handle ambiguity. This is the make-or-break round.

Roles That Are Always Open

Palantir hires in cycles tied to government contracts and commercial expansion, but these roles are consistently in demand:

  • Forward Deployed Software Engineer — the core role. Expect to relocate to a client site.
  • Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) — a broader title that includes data engineering, ML, and platform work
  • Business Development — finding and landing new government and commercial contracts
  • Forward Deployed Product Manager — Palantir's version of PM, embedded with clients

The Secret Weapon: Referrals

Palantir's hiring is heavily referral-driven. Their internal culture emphasizes "finding missionaries" — and the best way to find them is through people who already are missionaries. If you know someone at Palantir, a warm intro matters more than a polished resume.

If you don't have a referral, the next best thing is demonstrated impact. Palantir loves candidates who have:

  • Built something from 0 to 1
  • Worked in a high-stakes environment (government, defense, finance)
  • Published technical work or spoken at conferences
  • Shown evidence of being comfortable with ambiguity
