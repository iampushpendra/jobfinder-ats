Remote Roles That Are Always in Demand

Certain functions have consistently high remote volume across these companies:

  • Software Engineering — Backend and infrastructure roles have the most remote flexibility. Frontend and mobile tend to cluster in hubs.
  • Product Management — Remote PMs are common, but require timezone overlap with the primary engineering hub.
  • Developer Relations — Naturally remote-friendly. Many companies hire DevRel specifically to be distributed.
  • Support & Success — Customer-facing roles increasingly global and remote.
  • Design — Mixed. Some companies (Airbnb, GitLab) are remote-first for design. Others prefer hybrid.

How to Search for Remote Roles Efficiently

  1. Use the right keywords. On ATS boards, search for: "Remote," "Global," "Work from anywhere," or specific countries. Some companies tag remote roles differently than others.
  2. Check timezone requirements. A role listed as "Remote" might require Americas timezone overlap. Read the full posting before applying.
  3. Sort by date posted. Remote roles at top companies get hundreds of applications in the first 48 hours. Being early matters.
  4. Use a direct board. Our remote jobs page pulls from 13 company ATS boards simultaneously — no waiting for aggregators to catch up.

The "Remote" Application Advantage

Here's something counterintuitive: remote roles at top companies can actually be easier to get than in-office equivalents. Why? Geographic arbitrage. A company hiring remotely from a global pool may have less competition from local FAANG offices. A strong candidate in a smaller market can stand out more than the 500th applicant in San Francisco.

The key is applying fast and directly. Remote roles close quickly because volume is high. Use the company's own ATS board — not LinkedIn Easy Apply, not a job aggregator that lags by days.
