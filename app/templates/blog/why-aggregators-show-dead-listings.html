Why Job Aggregators Show Dead Listings (and How We Fix It)

October 28, 2026

You spend an hour tailoring your resume. You craft the perfect cover letter. You click apply — and the role closed three weeks ago. Or worse, it was never real in the first place. This isn't a bug. It's a business model.

The Incentive Problem

Job aggregators — the big names you know — make money from engagement, not outcomes. Their primary revenue comes from employers who pay to promote listings and from ads served to job seekers. More listings = more page views = more revenue. Whether those listings are current is secondary.

Here's how dead listings happen:

  • Scrape once, never update. Aggregators crawl company career pages periodically — sometimes weekly, sometimes monthly. A role that closed yesterday still shows as "active."
  • Employer ghost listings. Some companies leave roles posted even when they're not actively hiring — to build a talent pipeline, meet diversity optics, or keep their employer brand visible.
  • Expired API tokens. When a company switches ATS providers or changes their careers page, aggregators often keep serving cached data for weeks.
  • Deliberate bait. Some aggregators show attractive roles that don't exist to get you to create an account or upload a resume.

The Real Cost to Job Seekers

The average tech job seeker applies to 50–80 roles. If 20% of those are dead listings — a conservative estimate — that's 10–16 hours of wasted effort. More importantly, it erodes motivation. Nothing kills momentum like ghost applications.

Beyond time, there's an opportunity cost. The roles that are real and fresh get buried under pages of outdated listings. By the time you find them, they've already received 200+ applications.

How We Built Something Different

We don't scrape. We poll APIs directly — the same APIs that power the companies' own job boards.

  • Greenhouse API: We hit boards-api.greenhouse.io directly for companies like Airbnb, Cloudflare, and Stripe
  • Lever API: We query api.lever.co for Spotify, Palantir, and others
  • Ashby API: We connect to api.ashbyhq.com for OpenAI, Ramp, and more

When a company closes a role on their own board, it disappears from our feed within hours — not weeks. When they open a new one, it appears immediately. There is no middleman, no caching delay, no incentive to keep dead listings alive.

The Technical Difference

Here's what most job seekers don't know: ATS boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby) are designed to be the source of truth. They power the company's own careers page, their internal recruiting dashboard, and their application routing. When a role is marked "closed" in Greenhouse, it vanishes from the API response. Period.

Aggregators, by contrast, often store listings in their own database and update them on a schedule. That schedule might be daily, weekly, or "when we get to it." The result is a graveyard of roles that closed days or weeks ago but still show as "active" on the aggregator.

What You Can Do Right Now

  1. Apply on the company's own board. If you find a role on an aggregator, click through to the company's careers page and apply there instead. Your application gets tracked properly and your data stays structured.
  2. Check the posting date. If a "great" role has been open for 90+ days, be skeptical. Top companies fill competitive roles in 2–4 weeks.
  3. Use a direct-source board. Our job board polls company ATS APIs directly. Every listing links to the company's official apply page. If it's closed, it's gone from our feed.

The Bottom Line

Job searching is hard enough without applying to ghosts. The fix is simple: go to the source. Use the company's own ATS board, not a middleman that profits from listing volume regardless of accuracy. Your time is worth more than that.
